## What I do

I help founders and small product teams get their digital product work
finished. That means approved designs built in React, existing interfaces
improved, and components made reusable so the next screen is quicker than
the last.

We agree the most useful piece of work, I give you a fixed price, and I
build it.

Founders and small teams are who I talk to first. Agencies and designers
with clients of their own are welcome too, under their brand if they prefer.

IDEM, my own design system, is how I work and the proof that I can: 40 React
components in a versioned package that a working app installs. Clients buy
the finished work. They don't need to buy or understand the pipeline.

## How it's priced

- **A fixed price for a defined job.** I look at the files first, then
  quote. Most first jobs take a few days and sit around £1,500 to £3,000.
- **A day rate for capacity.** £600 a day, for a regular day a week,
  agency overflow, or a short contract. Small regular commitments are
  £80 an hour, sold as a monthly block of hours.
- **A component library**, when a team clearly needs one, is quoted after a
  small representative set has shown the fit. Expect £12,000 to £18,000.

Screens, states and components are fine ways to describe a job, as long as
the price accounts for how complex they are.

There's no retainer yet. After a finished job I ask what continuing help
would actually be useful, and price that.

## How the day rate works

£600 pays for a full day of client work: 7.5 hours. I invoice the days or
hours I actually work for the client, and a fixed-price job is invoiced at
its price, whatever the hours.

But a day of client work isn't a typical day. Over a year, only about 55%
of my working time is billable. The rest is sales, admin and keeping IDEM
sharp, on top of holidays, sick days and a Sabbath that doesn't move. So
£600 a billed day averages about £330 across every working day, which is
roughly what the practice needs to run. Behind each paid hour sit about
49 unpaid minutes.

£600 sits between the median and the upper quarter of UK contract rates
for React and design-system work, and well under agency rates for the
same seniority. The first client in this practice can have £525 a day for
three months, named as an introductory rate, then £600. That's
the deliberate, bounded generosity `OPERATING_MODEL.md` asks for, decided
before any client is in the room.

The working-time model behind these numbers is the ORIN Pricing sheet.

## Five rules

1. **Only take work I can deliver well.** A real need, someone who can
   approve decisions, and a budget that fits the work.
2. **Agree the result and price in writing.** One page: what's included,
   what counts as finished, what the client provides, and payment.
3. **Take a deposit before starting.** 50% upfront and 50% on completion for
   small jobs; milestones for larger ones.
4. **Price extra work before doing it.** One round of review is included.
   Fixing my own mistakes is always free. A new request gets a price.
5. **One main delivery project at a time**, with time kept for sales and
   admin.

Before a proposal, I give about two hours of free effort. If a job needs
more investigation than that, the investigation becomes a small paid job
with its own useful output.

## What doesn't change

How I behave is in `OPERATING_MODEL.md`: price honestly, give deliberately,
tell the truth without shaming anyone, keep Sabbath. The client owns what
they pay for and gets enough rights to run and maintain anything built with
my reusable tools.

## Reviewing this page

Prices and rules here are working assumptions, not doctrine. I review them
once a month against what buyers actually did: conversations, proposals,
deposits, revenue and hours. Change the weakest part and log it in
`decisions.md`.

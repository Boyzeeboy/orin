Principles

Orin builds design systems for teams shipping without a full design department. The work is technical, but the principles are simple: tell the truth, build the system, and leave the team stronger than you found it.

The system is the main thing

Most product debt does not begin in the screen. It begins underneath it: duplicated components, missing tokens, unclear ownership, and design decisions made again and again because there is no shared source of truth. Orin works at that layer first.

Design and development are one craft

Figma and code are usually treated as separate worlds, then everyone pays for the gap between them. Orin builds the pipeline between the two: tokens, components, page patterns, guardrails, and the working loop that keeps them connected.

Truth should not humiliate people

A good Diagnostic will expose drift, duplication, and waste. It should not make good people feel stupid. Developers are usually carrying design decisions the system should have made for them. The point is to name the pressure clearly, then remove it.

Evidence beats performance

Orin does not sell polish the system cannot support. Findings come from the product, the codebase, the Figma file, the token source, and the people doing the work. If the evidence says the problem is somewhere awkward, that is where the work starts.

The work should keep working after handoff

A design system that depends on a consultant remembering how everything fits together is already drifting. Orin leaves the system in the client's repo: tokens, generated outputs, reports, guardrails, and a working loop the team can run again without me.

Commercial clarity protects the work

Orin starts with bounded engagements, scopes the Build from evidence, and prices against outcomes rather than days. That protects the client from vague promises and protects the work from being squeezed into a shape that cannot hold.

If those principles fit the kind of partner you want, the Diagnostic or Foundation is where we start.
